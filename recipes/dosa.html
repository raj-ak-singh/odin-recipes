Description

Dosa is a popular South Indian crepe made from a fermented batter of rice and black lentils (urad dal).

The batter is thinly spread onto a hot, flat griddle and cooked until it achieves a beautifully golden brown, crispy exterior while remaining slightly soft on the inside.


This versatile dish is typically served hot alongside sambar (a flavorful lentil and vegetable stew) and a variety of chutneys, most commonly coconut and tomato-onion.

While plain dosa is a classic favorite, it is often stuffed with a spiced potato mixture to create the famous masala dosa, making it a hearty and beloved meal for breakfast or dinner across the globe.

Ingredients

  • Rice: A combination of regular raw white rice and parboiled rice (often sold as "dosa rice").
  • Urad Dal: Whole or split husked black lentils, which provide the soft, airy texture.
  • Fenugreek Seeds (Methi): A small spoonful to aid fermentation and add a signature aroma.
  • Water: Used for soaking the grains and blending them into a smooth batter.
  • Salt: Added after fermentation to enhance the flavor.

Steps

  1. Rinse and Soak
    1. Separate the ingredients: Place 4 cups of rice and 1 teaspoon of fenugreek seeds in one large bowl. Place 1 cup of urad dal in a separate bowl.
    2. Wash thoroughly: Rinse both bowls under cold running water 3 to 4 times until the water runs clear.
    3. Soak in water: Submerge both mixtures completely in fresh water. Let them soak for at least 4 to 6 hours.
  2. Blend into Batter
    1. Grind the dal: Drain the urad dal, reserving the soaking water. Blend the dal in a high-speed blender or wet grinder, adding a few tablespoons of the reserved water at a time, until it turns into a thick, frothy, and completely smooth paste. Pour into a large deep pot.
    2. Grind the rice: Drain the rice and fenugreek seeds. Blend them together with just enough fresh water to keep the blades moving. This paste should be smooth but can have a very slightly grainy texture.
    3. Combine: Pour the rice batter into the same deep pot with the dal paste.
  3. Ferment
    1. Mix by hand: Use clean hands to mix the two batters thoroughly for 2 to 3 minutes. Hand mixing introduces natural wild yeast needed for fermentation.
    2. Leave to rise: Cover the pot loosely with a lid. Place it in a warm, dark spot (like inside an turned-off oven with the light on) for 8 to 12 hours until the batter doubles in volume and smells pleasantly sour.
  4. Season and Cook
    1. Add salt: Gently fold 1.5 teaspoons of salt into the fermented batter without knocking out too much of the trapped air.
    2. Adjust consistency: The batter should be easily pourable, similar to pancake batter. Whisk in a little water if it feels too thick
