Description

Vada pav is an iconic vegetarian street food native to Mumbai, Maharashtra. Often called the "Bombay burger," it features a hot, deep-fried spiced potato fritter (known as a batata vada) tucked inside a soft, sliced bread roll called a pav.

The potato filling is seasoned with mustard seeds, turmeric, green chilies, garlic, and curry leaves, then dipped in a chickpea flour (besan) batter and fried until golden and crisp


Created in the 1960s near Mumbai's Dadar station by street vendor Ashok Vaidya, the snack was designed as a quick, portable, and affordable meal for the city's heavy textile mill workers.

Today, it is elevated by slathering the pav with tangy green chutney, sweet tamarind chutney, and a fiery, dry garlic-peanut chutney.

Ingredients

  • Potatoes: 4 large (boiled, peeled, and mashed)
  • Oil: 1 tablespoon
  • Mustard seeds: 1 teaspoon
  • Asafoetida (Hing): A pinch
  • Curry leaves: 7-8 leaves (finely chopped)
  • Ginger-Garlic-Green Chili Paste: 1 to 2 tablespoons (adjust chili to taste)
  • Turmeric powder: ½ teaspoon
  • Lemon juice: 1 tablespoon
  • Fresh coriander (Cilantro): 2-3 tablespoons (finely chopped)
  • Salt: To taste
  • Chickpea flour (Gram flour/Besan): 1 cup
  • Rice flour: 1 tablespoon (for extra crispiness)
  • Turmeric powder: ¼ teaspoon
  • Red chili powder: ¼ teaspoon
  • Baking soda: A tiny pinch (optional, for fluffiness)
  • Water: Around ½ cup (to make a thick, smooth batter)
  • Salt: To taste
  • Oil: For deep frying
  • Garlic: 10 - 12 cloves (peeled)
  • Grated dry coconut (Copra): 3-4 tablespoons
  • Roasted peanuts: 2 tablespoons
  • Kashmiri red chili powder: 1 to 2 tablespoons (for that deep red color)
  • Salt: To taste
  • Pav: 6 to 8 soft bread rolls
  • Green Chutney: Made from coriander, mint, green chilies, and lemon juice
  • Sweet Tamarind Chutney: Optional, for a hint of sweetness
  • Green Chilies: 5-6 whole chilies (fried and salted, served on the side)

Steps

  1. Make the Dry Garlic Chutney
    1. Roast ingredients: Heat a small pan on low heat. Dry roast the garlic cloves and grated dry coconut separately until they turn golden brown.
    2. Blend: Let them cool completely. Grind them in a blender along with the peanuts, Kashmiri red chili powder, and salt until it forms a coarse powder. Set aside.
  2. Prepare the Potato Filling (Batata Vada)
    1. Make the tempering: Heat 1 tablespoon of oil in a pan. Add the mustard seeds and let them splutter. Add the hing and curry leaves, and sauté for a few seconds.
    2. Cook spices: Add the ginger-garlic-green chili paste and turmeric powder. Sauté for about 1 minute until the raw smell disappears.
    3. Mix with potatoes: Turn off the heat. Add the mashed potatoes, salt, lemon juice, and chopped coriander leaves. Mix everything thoroughly.
    4. Shape: Once the mixture cools down, divide it into 6 to 8 equal portions and roll them into neat round balls.
  3. Mix the Batter & Fry the Vadas
    1. Whisk batter: In a mixing bowl, combine besan, rice flour, turmeric, red chili powder, salt, and a pinch of baking soda. Gradually add water and whisk into a smooth, thick batter (it should coat the back of a spoon).
    2. Heat oil: Heat oil for deep frying in a kadhai or deep pan on medium-high heat.
    3. Fry: Dip each potato ball into the batter, ensuring it is fully coated. Carefully drop it into the hot oil. Fry until the vadas turn golden yellow and crispy on all sides. Drain on paper towels.
  4. Assemble the Vada Pav
    1. Prep the bread: Slice a pav horizontally, keeping one side attached like a book.
    2. Apply chutneys: Spread a layer of green chutney on one inner side, and a generous amount of dry garlic chutney on the other side.
    3. Assemble: Place a hot, fried batata vada in the center, and gently press the pav closed.
    4. Serve: Serve immediately while hot with a side of fried, salted green chilies.
